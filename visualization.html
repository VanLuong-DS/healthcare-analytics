Visualization & Data Storytelling

A useful chart is not the one with the most detail. It is the one that helps your audience understand the point.

Start with the audience

Who will read this—analysts, clinicians, business leaders or a general audience? Decide what they need to know and what decision the analysis supports.

Choose the chart that fits

Bar: compare categories. Line: show change over time. Histogram: inspect distributions. Scatter: examine relationships. Table: show exact values. Avoid pie charts when many categories make comparison difficult.

Excel and PowerPoint

Excel is often enough for quick charts and pivot summaries. In PowerPoint, lead with the takeaway in the slide title and keep supporting details below. Remove decorative elements that compete with the data.

Power BI and interactive dashboards

Power BI Desktop is useful for building reports, measures and interactive filters. Sharing reports through the Power BI service can require paid licenses or eligible capacity depending on the setup. Check current licensing before publishing broadly.

Tell the story in a useful order

1. Executive summary: what changed and why it matters. 2. Key findings: two or three supported observations. 3. Evidence: charts and definitions. 4. Caveats: limitations. 5. Next steps: what should happen next.
