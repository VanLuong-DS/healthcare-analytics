Understanding U.S. Healthcare Data

Before analyzing healthcare data, it helps to understand who creates it, who pays for care and what each record actually represents.

The healthcare ecosystem

Patients receive care from providers such as physicians, hospitals and pharmacies. Payers—including commercial insurers and public programs—finance or administer coverage. Manufacturers develop medicines and distributors help move products through the supply chain.

Medical claims

A medical claim is an administrative record submitted for payment. Common fields include patient, provider, service date, diagnosis, procedure, payer, place of service, and amounts. A claim is not a complete clinical record.

Pharmacy claims

Pharmacy claims can include a dispensing date, drug identifier, quantity, days supply and payment details. A paid claim does not prove a patient took the medication.

Other data sources

Electronic health records can contain clinical notes, observations and lab results. Registries, surveys and public datasets add other perspectives. Each source has its own coverage and limitations.

Codes and identifiers

ICD-10-CM describes diagnoses, CPT/HCPCS describes many services, NDC identifies drug products, and NPI identifies healthcare providers. Codes change over time, and real analysis needs the correct version and context.

Define the patient journey carefully

A claims-based journey may follow observed diagnosis, visits and treatment fills. Enrollment gaps, delayed submissions, missing cash transactions and care outside the data can create apparent gaps that are not real care gaps.
