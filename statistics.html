Statistics That Help You Understand Data

You do not need advanced mathematics to start. These are the ideas I use most often when checking a dataset or explaining a result.

Start with the denominator

A count is useful, but a rate gives it context. If 40 of 200 patients had a visit, the share is 20%. Always ask: who is included, and over what time period?

Mean, median and percentiles

The mean is the total divided by the number of observations. The median is the middle value after sorting. The 90th percentile is a threshold at or below which about 90% of values fall. For skewed claims costs, the median can describe a typical claim better than the mean.

mean = sum(values) / len(values)
# median: middle value of sorted observations

Spread and outliers

Look at minimum, maximum, quartiles, standard deviation, and a histogram. A high average claim amount may be driven by a few very expensive visits.

Correlation is not causation

Two measures moving together does not prove that one caused the other. Consider patient mix, severity, eligibility, and missing data before drawing conclusions.

Sampling and uncertainty

A sample is only useful when you understand how it was selected. Confidence intervals communicate uncertainty; a p-value alone does not tell you whether a result matters in practice.

Try it yourself

Use the visits table to compare the mean and median allowed amount. Which is larger? What happens when you filter to paid claims?
